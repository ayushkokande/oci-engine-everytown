Monitoring and evaluation can start with a few consistent measures rather than a full evaluation
design. Pick three to five indicators that connect to your goals, such as participants enrolled,
contacts or sessions delivered, referrals completed, and one or two outcome measures you can
collect safely and consistently.

Use one shared tracking tool (a spreadsheet with locked columns is fine) and define each field so
every staff member records it the same way. Collect only what you will use, protect participants'
privacy, and get informed consent before using anyone's story externally.

Review the numbers monthly as a team: what went up, what went down, what surprised you, and what you
will try differently next month. This "learning loop" is the part funders value most, and it turns
data from a reporting burden into a management tool.

Once the basics are steady, consider partnering with a local university or evaluator to deepen the
analysis, and document your program model so results can be interpreted correctly.
