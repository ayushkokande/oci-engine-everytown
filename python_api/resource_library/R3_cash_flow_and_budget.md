title: Budgets, cash-flow projections and monthly financial review

An annual budget says what you plan to spend; a cash-flow projection says when money will actually
arrive and leave. Small organizations that rely on reimbursement-based government grants need both,
because a fully funded year can still run out of cash in a given month.

Build a 12-month cash-flow sheet: opening balance, expected receipts by month (with the realistic
payment date, not the award date), expected expenses by month, and closing balance. Update it monthly
and flag any month where the closing balance falls below one month of operating costs.

Hold a short monthly finance review with the executive director and the board treasurer or finance
committee: budget vs. actual, cash position, restricted vs. unrestricted funds, and upcoming
reporting deadlines. Keep restricted grant funds tracked separately so spending matches each
funder's terms.

As capacity grows, aim for an operating reserve (many organizations start with a target of one to
three months of expenses) and written basic financial policies.
